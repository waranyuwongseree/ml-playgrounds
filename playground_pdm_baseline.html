Five methods of building an expected bearing temperature are compared on one pump. The first tab shows only a stretch of history believed to be healthy, with the driving flow, the responding temperature and each method's residual, and asks which method actually explains this machine. The second tab keeps the same fit and lets the clock run into a period the method never saw, part of it still healthy and part of it carrying a fault, and scores every method on its own alarm limit in the operator's units: how many hours after the fault it first speaks, and how many times it cries wolf while the machine is still healthy.
